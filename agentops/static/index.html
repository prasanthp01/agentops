01 / THE PROBLEM

One request.
More than one constraint.

A low token price means little if a model cannot process the data in an approved region, meet the required quality, or return the right structure.

AgentOps gives application teams a shared policy boundary. An incident summary from ResolveAI, for example, would carry its data classification and spending limit. AgentOps checks eligible models, handles a provider failure, and records the attempts. The originating app retains its data, permissions, and approval process.

This deployed standalone service runs policy checks, fallback and usage accounting on synthetic requests. Service-to-service integrations with the other public demos are not enabled.

    04 / EVIDENCE, NOT ASSUMPTIONS
    What this build can demonstrate.
    Policy correctness
    Hard filters precede ranking. Tests exercise forbidden regions, capability mismatch, budget races, expired policies, and provider failures.
    Verification results are recorded in the local handoff.
    Recoverable operations
    Durable jobs, expiring leases, fenced writes, cancellation, and per-attempt deduplication. A caller keeps responsibility for its business workflow.
    No claim of exactly-once external effects.
    Honest economics
    Every mock attempt appears in the ledger. Modeled costs include failed attempts. External LLM spend is $0; infrastructure still has a cost.
    No live-provider performance or savings claim.
    Security boundaries & limitations
    Session ownership and CSRF protect public runs. Service credentials are scoped to one app. The API rejects arbitrary prompts, URLs, commands, extra fields, and anonymous live inference. The public engine has no outbound provider path.
    SQLite is designed here for a single host on local disk. This is a production-oriented engineering demonstration, not evidence of commercial production workloads or security certification. Live-model operation and cross-project integration are not enabled.
